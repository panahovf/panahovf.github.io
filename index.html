I am an economist and quantitative researcher. Most of my work has been building the analytical layer underneath Canadian public policy research — the energy transition and climate, autos and trade, competitiveness and growth. Usually that means taking a question nobody has good numbers for and finding a way to measure it.

I currently work with RBC's Thought Leadership team and Climate Action Institute, where I author, co-author and contribute to the flagship reports. I've also worked with the World Bank on measuring green employment in developing labour markets, and with Prof. Alissa Kleinnijenhuis on quantifying the costs and benefits of fossil-fuel phase-out globally.

I studied economics at UBC and hold a master's from MIT. In Toronto I sit on the board of the Toronto Association for Business and Economics (TABE), help run the MIT Alumni Club, and mentor through UBC's alumni program.

You can find me at the Madison Avenue Pub on Wednesdays, playing chess.
